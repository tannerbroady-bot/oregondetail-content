# When Is the Best Time of Year for Ceramic Coating in the Willamette Valley?

*By Tanner Broady, founder of Oregon Detail Co · November 4, 2024*

Any time of year works when the coating goes on indoors. The better question is what you want the coating to handle next. Here in the Willamette Valley, that's usually one of three things: the long wet season, spring pollen, or summer sun and bugs. Get the coating on before whichever one bothers you most.

## Why the weather outside doesn't decide it

A ceramic coating needs clean, dry paint to bond well, and it needs time to cure before it gets wet. Outdoors, that's hard to guarantee in Eugene, where the wet season runs from mid-October into early May.

At Oregon Detail Co, every coating is installed and cured inside our 4,000 sq ft shop on Conger St. The car is washed, decontaminated and polished indoors, coated panel by panel, then left to cure indoors for at least 12 hours. That's why every coating keeps the car overnight, and why rain in the forecast doesn't change the job.

## The valley, season by season

### Fall: before the wet season

For most daily drivers, this is the strongest case. From October on, cars spend months under road film, fir needles, sap and the de-icer that gets used on the passes. A coated finish sheds water and grime instead of holding it, so the car stays cleaner between washes and rinses off with far less effort. If this is your main worry, our guide to [protecting your paint before winter](https://oregondetail.com/blog/winter-paint-protection-oregon) goes into more detail.

### Late winter and early spring: before pollen season

Spring in the valley means a yellow film on every car, plus sap from the trees most of us park under. On coated paint, pollen and sap don't stick as hard, so they come off in a normal wash instead of sitting on the clear coat for weeks.

### Early summer: before the sun and bugs

Our summers are dry and bright, and July brings bug season on every highway drive. A coating adds a layer of UV protection and makes bug splatter much easier to wash off before it has time to stain.

### The middle of winter

Coating a car in January works just as well as coating it in July, because the whole process happens indoors. If your car is new to you or just came out of storage, there's no reason to wait for spring.

## Plan around the first week

Wait at least seven days before the first wash. After that, hand wash with a pH-neutral soap and a microfiber mitt. Touchless washes are fine, but skip automatic washes with brushes. If you're coating ahead of a road trip, leave a little room so the first week isn't spent on a bug-covered highway.

Not outdoors. At our shop it can, because the car is prepped, coated and cured inside, so rain and cold outside don't affect the job.

### Is fall or spring the better time?

Fall, if your main concern is the wet season and road grime. Late winter, if pollen and sap bother you most. Either way, the coating keeps working for years, so the timing matters most for the first season.
