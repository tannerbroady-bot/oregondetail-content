# Single-Stage Paint on Classic Cars: What a Detailer Can Safely Do

*By Tanner Broady, founder of Oregon Detail Co · November 13, 2026*

A lot, if it's done carefully. Single-stage paint can usually be cleaned, decontaminated, polished back to a deep gloss and protected. But it's often thin and decades old, and every polishing pass removes a little of it. The goal is the most improvement for the least paint removed, and sometimes the right call is to leave a flaw where it is.

## What single-stage paint is

Most modern cars have a base coat of color covered by a separate clear coat. Single-stage paint puts the color and the gloss in one layer, with no clear coat on top. Most cars built before the 1980s left the factory this way, and many classics still wear that original finish.

That changes how the paint behaves. When single-stage paint oxidizes, the color itself goes dull and chalky, because there's no clear layer above it taking the weather. And any polishing works directly on the color.

### How to tell if your car has it

Put a little polish or cleaner wax on a white applicator and work it on an inconspicuous spot. If the pad picks up the car's color, the paint is single-stage. On a clear-coated car the pad stays white or picks up only dirt.

## What a careful detailer does

### 1\. Clean gently

A careful hand wash and decontamination come first, so grit isn't dragged across soft, thin paint.

### 2\. Measure

Older paint has often been polished before, sometimes many times. Measuring the paint thickness, where the body material allows it, shows how much is left to work with before any machine touches it.

### 3\. Test a small area

We start with a test spot using the gentlest combination that might work, then step up only if we have to. The test shows how the paint responds and how much correction it can safely give.

### 4\. Correct only as much as the paint can give

On thin or original paint, the right answer is often a big improvement in gloss and clarity with some deeper marks left alone. Heavier steps like wet sanding are only for paint with enough depth, and only after measuring and testing.

### 5\. Protect it

Freshly polished single-stage paint needs protection. A good wax or sealant adds depth and keeps oxidation from coming back. A ceramic coating is an option for some classics, and we'll talk it through once we've seen the car.

## Why preservation comes first

Original paint can't be replaced. Once it's polished through, the only fix is a repaint, and for many classics that changes what the car is. Collectors and judges value originality, so on an original car we treat the paint as something to preserve, not just make shiny.

## Frequently asked questions

### Can oxidized single-stage paint be saved?

Often, yes, as long as there's enough paint left. A test spot shows how much gloss comes back and how much correction the paint can safely take.

### Should I polish my classic myself?

A gentle hand wash and a non-abrasive wax are safe. Machine polishing on thin original paint is where most of the damage happens, so measure and test before you start, or have it done by someone who will.
