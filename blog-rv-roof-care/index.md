# RV Roof Care in Oregon: A Year-Round Schedule for Wet Winters

*By Tanner Broady, founder of Oregon Detail Co · September 1, 2024*

Clean and protect your RV's roof once a year, ideally in late summer or early fall before the rain starts. Keep it clear of needles and debris through the wet season, then wash and wax the rest of the coach in spring. If your RV sits outside year-round, check the roof more often. Here's the schedule we recommend to Oregon owners.

## Why the roof matters more in Oregon

An RV roof spends months wet here. Fir needles, leaves and road grime collect on it, hold water against the surface and slowly wash down the sides as black streaks. Then summer brings strong UV that dries out and dulls whatever is left unprotected. Most owners never see the roof, so problems build up quietly until the streaks or chalky paint show up on the sides.

## A year-round roof schedule

### Late summer to early fall: clean and protect

This is the one appointment that matters most. Our [Roof Cleaning & Protection](https://oregondetail.com/rv-services) service is a yearly deep clean built for exactly this:

- Roof pressure washed and scrubbed to remove dirt, pollutants and grime
- Roof dried after cleaning
- A 1-year UV-resistant sealant with a clean matte finish

A clean, sealed roof going into the wet season means less grime running down the sides all winter. It's priced at $7–$10 per foot, about $210–$300 for a 30-foot RV.

### Through the wet season: keep it clear

- After windstorms, clear branches, needles and leaves before they mat down and hold water.
- Look over the seams and the sealant around vents, skylights and the AC unit. If you see cracking or gaps, have an RV service center repair it before the next storm.
- Check your owner's manual before you climb up. Not every roof is rated to walk on.
- A cover or covered storage cuts down on all of this if you have the option.

### Spring: wash and wax the coach

Once the rain lets up, wash off a winter's worth of road film and protect the paint before summer. Our RV Wash & Wax ($15–$20 per foot) includes a roof rinse, a full hand wash and a paint sealant. If the paint has already gone chalky, the Elite Coach Package ($30–$40 per foot) adds machine paint correction to remove oxidation.

### Summer: on the road

The UV-resistant sealant from your roof service is doing its job now. A basic RV Wash ($7–$10 per foot, roof rinse included) after a long trip keeps bugs and road grime from baking on.

## What black streaks are telling you

Those dark streaks running down from the roof line are dirt and residue from the roof being carried down the sides by rain. Washing the sides removes them for a while, but they come back until the roof itself is cleaned and protected. That's why we treat the roof as a yearly service, not an afterthought.
